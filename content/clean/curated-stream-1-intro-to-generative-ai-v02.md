## Page 2

### What are generative AI tools?

Generative AI tools are advanced systems that create new
outputs — text, images, code, audio, video — based on the
inputs you give them.
They act like a versatile virtual assistant: helping you write,
brainstorm, summarise, analyse, plan, and learn — across
almost every industry.
In 2026 the most familiar names are ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude,
and Copilot — but the field is much wider, and growing every
month.

## Page 12

### More detail = better answers

To get the response you want, give the tool as much useful context as you can: who you are, what you want, and any
constraints.
Vague prompt
“Tell me about Nelson.”
- Generic list of facts
- May miss what you actually need
- Doesn't match your situation
Detailed prompt
“We're in Nelson, NZ for three days. We love the outdoors
and want to visit some restaurants and museums. Suggest a
balanced itinerary.”
- Tailored itinerary that fits your time
- Mix of activities — outdoors + food + culture
- Often groups by day or theme

## Page 13

### Don't stop at the first answer

1. Ask
Start with your best version of the
question.
2. Review
Read the answer. What's missing?
What's off?
3. Refine
Add detail, change scope, or ask a
follow-up.
Repeat until the answer is useful. This is called iterating.
A reminder that's worth repeating
These models generate what “seems” right based on patterns — they don't always know what's true. Always sanity-check anything that matters
before you use it.

### Test the limits with a fresh question

These prompts often reveal differences in how each tool handles very recent or local information:
Prompt A — current info
“What are five restaurants open in Nelson today
for me to eat at?”
Prompt B — official number
“What is the current Official Cash Rate in New
Zealand?”
What to look for
- Does the tool admit it doesn't know, or guess?
- Does it cite a source or web link?
- Does it offer to look it up rather than answer from memory?

## Page 16

### Web-connected vs. offline answers

Most 2026 assistants can browse the web when needed, but each one decides differently. Older free tiers might not browse at all.
Connected to the web
Will fetch fresh information for things like opening hours, latest
news, or live prices.
- Gemini — always web-connected by default
- ChatGPT (2026) — browses when it's useful
- Claude — can use web search; opt-in for some plans
Answering from training
Will answer from what it learnt during training. Great for general
knowledge, but can be out of date or make things up.
- Useful for: writing, brainstorming, explaining concepts
- Risky for: prices, dates, news, opening hours
- Always sanity-check facts that matter

## Page 17

### When AI “makes things up”

Hallucination
When an AI gives an answer that sounds confident
and reasonable — but is partly or completely wrong.
It happens because these models predict what words
usually come next — they don't “know” facts the way you
do.
All current models — Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini — can hallucinate.
They are getting better, but never zero.
Common ways it shows up
Made-up facts
Quoting a statistic, date or law that doesn't exist.
Fake citations
Inventing a book, paper or article title that sounds real.
Confident guessing
Filling gaps with plausible-sounding detail.
Old information
Treating last-year facts as if they're still current.
