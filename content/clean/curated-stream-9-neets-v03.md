### Step-by-step: draft a CV in ChatGPT

ChatGPT is free at chatgpt.com. You can use it without signing in for short tasks — but signing up keeps your chats saved.
1
Open chatgpt.com
Click 'Sign up' (it's free) or use it without
signing in.
2
Type your first prompt
Try: 'Can you help me write a CV? I'm 18
and applying for retail jobs in Nelson.'
3
Answer its questions
It'll ask about school, jobs, skills. Type
what you can — leave the rest blank.
4
Read the draft
ChatGPT will lay out a CV. Check spelling,
dates, and anything that sounds 'off'.
5
Ask for changes
'Make the summary sound more like me.'
'Add a hobbies section.' Keep tweaking.
6
Copy into a doc
Paste it into Google Docs or Word. Save
as PDF when you're done.
Pro tip: tell ChatGPT what job you're applying for. It'll tailor the wording to that role.

### A starter prompt you can copy

Paste this into ChatGPT to begin. Change the bits in [square brackets] to match you.
P R O M P T
Please help me write a CV. I'm [age] and I live in [city/town in NZ].
I'm applying for [type of job, e.g. retail / hospitality / labouring / care work].
Here's what I've done:
- School: [last school, NCEA level]
- Work / volunteer: [list what you've done — even if short]
- Things I'm good at: [e.g. being on time, talking to customers, lifting heavy things, te reo]
- Interests: [hobbies, sports, gaming]
Please write the CV in plain New Zealand English. Keep it to one page.
What happens next?
ChatGPT will likely ask a few follow-up questions — that's a good thing. Answer them in plain words. After two or three back-and-forths you'll
have a usable draft.

## Page 10

### Two things to watch for

AI is good but not magic. These two things catch a lot of people out.
It can make things up
ChatGPT can invent jobs, achievements, dates, or
qualifications that aren't yours. This is called a 'hallucination'.
Always read your CV line-by-line. If something isn't true,
delete it.
It can sound too 'corporate'
Words like 'leveraged synergies' or 'spearheaded' don't sound
like a real person. Ask: 'rewrite this in plain New Zealand
English, like a 17-year-old would say it.'
Golden rule: your CV is YOU. Read every single line out loud. If you wouldn't say it that way in real life, change it.

## Page 14

### What a good cover letter actually says

Four short paragraphs. That's it. The whole letter fits on one page.
1. Why you're writing
'I'm applying for the retail assistant job advertised on Trade Me Jobs.'
One sentence.
2. Why you'd be good at it
Two or three things from your CV that match the job. 'I love talking to
people, I'm reliable, and I've helped on the front desk at my school.'
3. Why this place
One line about why you want to work HERE specifically. 'I shop at
your Nelson store and the team always seems friendly.'
4. The wrap-up
'Thanks for considering my application. I'd be keen to talk it through if
you'd like to meet.' Sign off with your name.
Keep it under 250 words. Hiring managers skim. The right four short paragraphs beat a long, padded letter every time.

### Questions to practise out loud

Q 1
Tell me a bit about yourself.
Q 2
Why do you want this job?
Q 3
What are you good at?
Q 4
Tell me about a time you worked in a

Q 5
How would your last teacher or

Q 6
How do you handle it when things go

Q 7
Why have you got a gap on your CV?
Q 8

Question 7 — the gap question — is the one most NEET young people get asked. Have a short, honest answer ready.

## Page 18

### Answering 'why is there a gap?'

You don't have to share more than you want to. Most employers just want to know you're ready now. Three honest ways to answer:
Health or family
'I took some time out to deal with some
personal stuff. That's sorted now, and I'm
keen to get into work.' Brief, honest, no
detail required.
Tried something that didn't fit
'I started a course but it wasn't the right fit,
so I stepped away to figure out what I
actually wanted to do. I'm clearer now.'
Still figuring it out
'I've spent the last while learning some new
skills [name one or two — driving, te reo, AI
tools]. Now I'm ready to put them to use.'
A S K C L A U D E O R C H A T G P T
"I'm 19 and I've been out of work / school for about [time]. Help me write 3 short, honest ways to answer 'why
is there a gap on your CV?' in a job interview. Keep them friendly and confident."

## Page 20

### Stay safe — and keep it honest

AI tools are powerful, but a few simple habits keep you (and your job application) on the right side.
Don't paste private stuff
No bank details, IRD numbers, or
passwords into a chatbot. Treat it like a
public space.
Keep it true
Never invent qualifications or jobs on your
CV. Employers do check, and fibs come
undone in interviews.
Write in your voice
Use AI to draft and tidy. Then read it aloud
and fix anything that doesn't sound like you.
Double-check facts
AI can make mistakes — names, dates,
even the name of the company. Always
cross-check.
Strong passwords
Use a different password for each AI
account. Turn on two-step sign-in if it's
offered.
Ask a real person too
AI is helpful, but a careers coach, librarian
or Youth Service worker is even better.
